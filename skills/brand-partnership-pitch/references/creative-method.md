# Creator fit and script craft

## Evidence before imitation

Understand how the channel works, not just its niche or follower count. Analyze:

- Format and structure: monologue, demonstration, montage, tutorial, ensemble sketch, narrative, documentary, interview. Is the structure interruptible?
- Opening mechanism: question, contradiction, visual reveal, immediate action, problem, punchline setup. Observe the opening rather than defaulting to "Wait, you need to see this".
- Rhythm: approximate shot length, pause placement, density of speech, repetitions, payoff timing. Distinguish measured timestamps from impressions.
- Camera and edit grammar: framing, movement, inserts, captions, locations, transitions, whether the creator appears at all.
- Voice: actual vocabulary, sentence length, audience address, humor mechanics. Extract patterns without copying distinctive chunks of source dialogue.
- Cast dynamics: who initiates, reacts, interrupts or resolves; solo vs ensemble; use named speakers only when names are established by explicit source context.
- Commercial behavior: observed sponsor entrances/exits, product use, disclosures, character continuity. Write "not observed" when appropriate; do not invent a track record.
- Relevance and production capacity: what this creator can credibly discuss and film with the people, places and equipment available.

For each claim store an observation, source URL/file, timestamp/range, and confidence grounded in the sample. Avoid definitive claims about the audience's demographics or purchase intent without data. A video's engagement does not prove why it performed well.

## Brief-to-creator connection

Extract objective, audience, required facts, acceptable formats, must-include/must-avoid items, usage context, CTA, language and resources. Distinguish facts from requested creative premises. A product URL is a source to read, not permission to invent missing specifications.

Choose a topic where the creator's established credibility overlaps with the actual user need. A natural use situation, a recurring problem or an in-character conflict is stronger than a generic product endorsement.

For sponsorships choose among integration, dedicated content and standalone ad according to the brief and creator format. Short narratives may not tolerate an inserted sponsor monologue; long modular videos can. Do not promise reach or repeat fixed algorithm percentages. Include disclosure wording supplied by the user; if jurisdiction-specific compliance is requested, verify current authoritative guidance instead of asserting the script is legally approved.

For organic content, tutorials and drama, use their actual objective: teach, entertain, explain, tell a story. Do not add a sales CTA or brand section when the user did not request a sponsorship.

## Write performable material

An alternative is a different premise/structure, not three paraphrased hooks. Recommend on fit, clarity, originality and filming effort with a short rationale.

- Audio is exact spoken dialogue, with speaker attribution for ensembles. Put intended silence, music or sound cues in brackets. Never use "creator explains the benefits" as dialogue.
- Visual is concrete stage action and camera instruction. State what is in frame, who acts and what changes. Avoid "make an engaging montage" without its shots.
- Use product details only when sourced. Do not invent ownership, personal experience, before/after results, testimonials or guarantees.
- Match screen orientation and physical use to reality. Show the actual product/brand only from supplied assets or verified references; generic illustrations must not pretend to reproduce exact UI.
- Time codes use seconds from the beginning and form a contiguous timeline. Reserve real time for reaction, action, pauses and transitions. Estimate speech from the actual language and creator, then read it aloud/mentally at natural speed.
- Scene count follows content. Full-length integrations contain all organic dialogue and sponsor material, not placeholders. For very long work, write chapter by chapter to files and review the complete assembled result.
- New ideas may depart from the observed formula intentionally; explain why, and do not label the new element as an observed channel habit.

Finish with a practical shoot checklist and a requirements review stating where mandatory items appear. These are editorial judgments, not legal certification or automated performance scores.
